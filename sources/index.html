research behind Moth

sources

I wanted the facts in Moth to be things you can actually trust, not random trivia copied from wherever. I used stronger sources whenever I could: universities, museums, government and conservation organizations, species databases, formal moth references, and peer-reviewed research for narrower claims.

What this page is: a public directory of the sources behind Moth's facts. Some are broad species or taxonomy references; others back up one specific claim or study. I try to use universities, museums, government and conservation organizations, established databases, and peer-reviewed research when they're the best fit.

peer-reviewed research

journal articles and primary studies

If a fact is really specific, I try to find the actual study behind it instead of stretching a general species page to fit. That includes things like echolocation defenses, migration, courtship chemistry, pollination, or something found in one experiment. If one study found something, I say that. I don't pretend it applies everywhere.

how I check a fact

  • I prefer species-level evidence over family- or genus-level generalizations.
  • If a fact comes from one experiment, locality, or rearing study, I try to phrase it that way instead of pretending it is universal.
  • I cross-check scientific names and synonyms rather than assuming an older name is still current.
  • I avoid padding a moth with weak trivia just to hit a number. Fewer strong facts are better than filler.
  • If wording makes an animal sound worse, grosser, or more dramatic than the biology actually is, I rewrite it.

found something wrong?

If you spot a fact that's wrong or a source that should be revisited, email report@mothselfcare.com. I want corrections to be easy to make.
