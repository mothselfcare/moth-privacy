research behind Moth

sources

I wanted the facts in Moth to be things you can actually trust, not random trivia copied from wherever. I used stronger sources whenever I could: universities, museums, government and conservation organizations, species databases, formal moth references, and peer-reviewed research for narrower claims.

What this page is: a public directory of the main sources used during Moth's research and fact-checking. It is not meant to imply that every card came from every source below, and it is not a one-source-per-card bibliography. Species-specific facts were checked against the strongest relevant source I could find, and narrow behavior claims were treated more cautiously than general life-history information.

peer-reviewed research

journal articles and primary studies

For unusually specific behavior—such as echolocation defenses, migration and navigation, courtship chemistry, pollination, or a result tied to one experiment—I used peer-reviewed or primary research when a broad species profile was not enough. Those claims were worded to match the strength and scope of the evidence rather than turning one study into a universal rule.

how I check a fact

  • I prefer species-level evidence over family- or genus-level generalizations.
  • If a fact comes from one experiment, locality, or rearing study, I try to phrase it that way instead of pretending it is universal.
  • I cross-check scientific names and synonyms rather than assuming an older name is still current.
  • I avoid padding a moth with weak trivia just to hit a number. Fewer strong facts are better than filler.
  • I also avoid negative or misleading framing when a more accurate, neutral way to describe the same biology exists.

found something wrong?

If you spot a factual mistake or a source that should be revisited, email report@mothselfcare.com. I want corrections to be easy to make.
