our story

why i made Moth

I wanted somewhere I could go when I felt like scrolling, but didn't wanna spend an hour doing it and then realize I basically got nothing out of that time.

the idea

I wanted something that was actually nice to scroll through. Somewhere I could read an affirmation, learn a random fact or a new word, do a little quest, write something down, or just look around for a few minutes and enjoy myself.

I also really didn't want it to feel annoying to use. I didn't want constant pop-ups, a bunch of pressure, or the feeling that everything good was sitting behind another screen asking you to pay. I wanted Moth to feel cozy and simple, like somewhere you'd actually want to come back to.

It started pretty small, mostly moths, affirmations, and some quests. Then I kept working on it and making more for it. I wrote the affirmations and quests, put together the facts and vocabulary, made the collections, came up with the moth discoveries and little features, and kept building everything around the kind of app I wanted Moth to be.

Over time it turned into a lot more than the original idea, but I still want it to feel simple when you actually use it. Somewhere you can scroll for a bit, learn something, reflect, maybe do something good for yourself, and feel like the time you spent there actually gave you something back.

why moths?

Honestly, I just really love moths.

I feel like they get such an undeserved bad reputation. They aren't dirty creatures or something to be scared of. They're pollinators, and there are so many beautiful and weird species. They're fascinating little creatures.

Butterflies get so much love, and it's deserved because they're beautiful. I just think moths are beautiful too, and deserve some of that love as well.

That's why they ended up becoming such a big part of the app. Every moth you can discover in Moth is a real species. You can collect them, learn about them, and hopefully start seeing why I like them so much too.

Moth is made by one person. I've built it, written it, designed the experience, and put all of these little pieces together myself. I'm basically just trying to make the kind of app I'd genuinely want to use.
